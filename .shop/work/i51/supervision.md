# supervision

The drafts of this proposal are in .shop/work/i51/runs/supervision/9-integrate/drafts/round-9/.

## Questions
- Question 1 (r174, r175; replace retired r162, r163): what does "mentions the glossary" mean?
  r174 (added, replaces r162): **When** a question of an initiative mentions the glossary, `work run` shall leave the question for the person, giving it to no session under a delegation.
  r175 (added, replaces r163): **Where** the request an initiative fulfils gives a delegation, `work run` shall give each question of the initiative that does not mention the glossary to a session of its own, given the delegation, the files that one question names and nothing else, which answers the question if the delegation covers it and leaves it for the person otherwise.
  Retired: r162 (names a term of the glossary or a glossary-proposal), r163 (names neither).
  Readings:
  (a) [recommended] The question's text names the glossary itself, which r176 makes every question about a term do; lines stand as drafted.
  (b) The question is about the glossary: it names the glossary, a term of it or a glossary-proposal. r174 would read: "**When** a question of an initiative names the glossary, a term of the glossary or a glossary-proposal, `work run` shall leave the question for the person, giving it to no session under a delegation." and r175 "... that names neither the glossary, a term of the glossary nor a glossary-proposal ..."
- Question 2 (r176): who does "a round or a worker" cover?
  r176 (added): **When** a round or a worker writes a question on the definition of a term or on a proposed term, the round or the worker shall name the glossary in the question.
  Readings:
  (a) [recommended] Only rounds and workers `work run` starts (the glossary's worker is a session for one stage of one work-item); as drafted.
  (b) Also the branch reviewer and answering sessions. r176 would read: "**When** a session that `work run` started writes a question on the definition of a term or on a proposed term, the session shall name the glossary in the question."
