# The grammar refuses a line with bad English: a verb missing its object or recipient, a condition missing its agent

## Notes

Readability is the bar, and a sentence a reader finds hard because its English is wrong is refused; 'a caller with the YouTube access of a viewer' is the case to weigh next. Refusing these breaks feed's r1, r2 and r4 and this repository's own lines that use HappensI, Do or a relational condition: the change names them and how each is reworded.
The grammar starts from basic English sentence structure, restricted: a clause is a subject, a verb and the nouns the verb takes; a glossary labels each term with its part of speech and gives each verb its frame (the kinds of its subject, object and recipient, or none), and a line that leaves out a noun its verb takes is refused. A condition that relates two kinds is a verb between them, and 'not' goes on a verb or a relation. Each verb's frame gives the facts model logic reasons with, so a line is logical, not only unambiguous. Every line of this repository is reworded to the new grammar in the same item; other contexts reword theirs after upgrading. ## Requirements

### design-model-check

- **objectless** (condition of requirement): a requirement with a verb whose glossary entry gives it an object, used with no object.
- **recipientless** (condition of requirement): a requirement with a verb whose glossary entry gives it a recipient, used with no recipient.
- **frameless** (condition of term): a verb whose glossary entry does not name the kinds of its subject and object, nor say that it takes no object.
- **relational** (condition of term): a condition whose definition names a kind other than the kind it is a condition of.
- **negating** (condition of requirement): a requirement that says with `not` that a verb or a relation between two kinds does not hold.
