# A published contract says how its operations behave

## Notes

C-1 of the contracts design, first slice. The published contract form, contract.toml: objects, operations with parameters, outcomes and reasons, the governing requirements spec annotate gathers, events, and the properties idempotent by, compensated by, followed by and delivery, written on the model block's operation and event lines (decision 0117).

## Requirements

### checking

#### [g64] A contract is prepared in the contract form
- [r64] When the person prepares a contract, the specification service shall return a contract that conforms to the contract form and change nothing.

#### [g65] A contract holds the requirements that govern its operations
- [r65] While a requirement governs an operation, when the person prepares a contract, the specification service shall return a contract that holds the requirement.

### specification model

```model
object contract kind of artifact
  relations: holds → operation | property | requirement (many)

object contract form kind of artifact

object property kind of text
  relations: refers to → operation (many)

object operation
  relations: has → property (many)

service specification
  operation prepare(contract)  outcomes: returned → the contract
```

- **contract** (noun) → object contract. What this bounded context publishes at a version in its own words, `contract.toml`: its objects, its operations with their parameters, outcomes and reasons, the requirements that govern each operation, its events, and the properties of its operations and events. Plural: contracts.
- **the contract form** (proper noun) → object contract form. The form of a contract's `contract.toml`: a table of each object, each operation with its parameters, outcomes, reasons, governing requirements and properties, and each event with its properties.
- **property** (noun) → object property. What an operation or an event promises beyond its outcomes, written on its model line: `idempotent by <parameter>`, `compensated by <operation>`, `followed by <event>`, or an event's `delivery` and `when`. Plural: properties.
- **refer** (verb, state) → relation property.refers to. A property names an operation of the domain model, as `compensated by <operation>` does. Forms: refers, referred. Frame: <property> refers to <operation>.
- **prepare** (verb, action) → operation specification.prepare. Forms: prepares, prepared. Frame: <actor> prepares <contract>.
