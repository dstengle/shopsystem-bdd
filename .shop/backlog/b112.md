# Review the controlled language's grammar: sentence structures over coined adjectives, balancing parsing, clarity and verbosity

## Notes

The person, 2026-11-08, answering a question on b107's r73 (an answer to a question, so no work quote): "We should review the underlying grammar of the CNL and make sure it is constructed in ways that balance parsing easily and reading/writing clarity vs verbosity (or other factors)." and "A grammar is sentence structure combined with terms. We should not be defining simple english terms with domain specific meaning." and "Do we have a set of base grammar that allows statements to be made using a restricted set of linguistic structures that yields unambiguous statements about the relationships between subjects and objects."
What prompted it: a condition (`While C`) is built from a noun and an adjective term, a relation row, a verb clause or a held resource; anything else becomes a coined one-word adjective with a prose definition (142 of 167 conditions here are prose; feed's `quiet` was coined only because the grammar has no relative clause). Examples the person questioned: quiet ("no video is from a channel that the viewer subscribes to": no video in what context), listless (its definition, "a result gives no change list", parses as a requirement's condition itself), unreadable, idle (r19 of migration-tools: a reader cannot tell how a repository is idle). The person's direction: common English words keep their English meaning; a small fixed set of general adjectives (empty, given, defined) may be universal; the structures (subject-verb-object frames, relative clauses, whose, quantifier scope) carry the meaning, not the glossary. b107 (relative clauses) and b10 (define the prose conditions) wait on this review.

Brainstorming of 2026-11-08 (the person: research, experimentation and prototyping, maybe a major release). Settled so far: definitions (types, "is a", relations) are timeless vocabulary; requirements are effect rules, structural rules, state invariants, transition rules and liveness, each a premise of the logic with its own verification route (scenarios, a check, judgement through a design-decision, runtime monitors by sampling); only ubiquitous lines need a route, carried by the decision (a `verified_by` beside `enforced_by`), never inside the sentence; a coined adjective is a state missing its structure (r33's `unreadable` becomes "a request comment does not conform to the comment schema"); formats live in external artifacts (a schema) that a line names and conforms to, read in as facts where a line needs their fields. Research plan, each phase a record: (1) CNL best practices and the field (Kuhn's PENS survey, Attempto, SBVR, EARS, FRET, GF's CNLs) beside a write-up of this grammar's underpinnings; (2) a sample of this context's lines and its grammar compared with those systems; (3) the full set of lines and conditions sorted by the structure that would replace each workaround.

Research done 2026-11-08, three records: .shop/records/2026-11-08-cnl-research-phase-1.md (the field: PENS, ACE, SBVR, PENG-ASP, FRET, EARS, Dwyer patterns, GF/RGL; this CNL end to end), -phase-2.md (27 lines rewritten, read and parsed by a prototype RGL grammar: all 27 parse, 26 to one tree), -phase-3.md (all 265 lines and 170 adjectives sorted). Findings for the design: the work is in the noun phrase (of, no X, has, relative clauses, whose: most rewrites); 137 lines lose a coined adjective; 66 of 170 adjectives are dead; this repository's lines are 250 of 265 next-step effects or refusals, so time, modality and monitors must be sized from a consumer such as feed; a DRS-like logical form with written reading rules (the, no, a in While, only) and indexicals (the run's repository, command, session) is what makes entailment and one meaning for check and Gherkin possible; verbs split into states and actions. Open choices for the person: a second frame per verb or a second verb (56 lines want a second sense; b84 settled one Frame per verb); design-model-check's 47 meta-level lines written out or as a named rule catalogue a line says a thing violates; Where for command options or an argument in the trigger; shortened refusals with U11 entailing the rest. Bugs found on the way: b113-b117, and the line faults in the item after b117.

Settled in the brainstorming, 2026-11-09: layers (a domain-free base grammar; importable term packs such as CLI and HTTP, each bringing words with their logical meaning; the product's terms; the bounded context's terms); a context may redefine a word, marked on its entry (Redefines:), and seam patterns decide how words cross contexts; compounds are attribute-derived (batch status), relation-derived (request comment) or named and declared (pull request), and an attributive noun is the way to tell different things apart (http status, work item); a verb may have several frames, its subject and object kinds picking the meaning (one meaning with a wider frame shares one predicate; different meanings told apart by kinds are separate predicates; two meanings with the same kinds need different words, refused otherwise), which partly reverses b84's one Frame per verb; design-model-check's lines name model check's rules from a catalogue artifact (b123, b124). Performance is not a constraint on the design (b119's studies).

Settled 2026-11-09 (continued): command options and API parameters are arguments in the trigger ("When the person runs `work backlog` with the `--json` option"), and Where is kept for product features; refusal lines say "shall refuse the command and name X", the rest entailed by U11 and the CLI pack's refuse (b122), shown to readers by an explain command (b126) and in the generated Gherkin, carried into the implementation and tests once (b125).

Settled 2026-11-09 (time): the first version covers time as domain data (time values and durations as attributes, units, after/before/within the last N, newest/oldest: function words in the base grammar, units in a time pack), time as a trigger (a clock actor in the time pack, schedules and moments as events) and the order of domain events (after/before/since over events, history "has published"); operational timing (within N, always, eventually) is deferred to b127.
