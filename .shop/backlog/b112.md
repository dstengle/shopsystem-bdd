# Review the controlled language's grammar: sentence structures over coined adjectives, balancing parsing, clarity and verbosity

## Notes

The person, 2026-11-08, answering a question on b107's r73 (an answer to a question, so no work quote): "We should review the underlying grammar of the CNL and make sure it is constructed in ways that balance parsing easily and reading/writing clarity vs verbosity (or other factors)." and "A grammar is sentence structure combined with terms. We should not be defining simple english terms with domain specific meaning." and "Do we have a set of base grammar that allows statements to be made using a restricted set of linguistic structures that yields unambiguous statements about the relationships between subjects and objects."
What prompted it: a condition (`While C`) is built from a noun and an adjective term, a relation row, a verb clause or a held resource; anything else becomes a coined one-word adjective with a prose definition (142 of 167 conditions here are prose; feed's `quiet` was coined only because the grammar has no relative clause). Examples the person questioned: quiet ("no video is from a channel that the viewer subscribes to": no video in what context), listless (its definition, "a result gives no change list", parses as a requirement's condition itself), unreadable, idle (r19 of migration-tools: a reader cannot tell how a repository is idle). The person's direction: common English words keep their English meaning; a small fixed set of general adjectives (empty, given, defined) may be universal; the structures (subject-verb-object frames, relative clauses, whose, quantifier scope) carry the meaning, not the glossary. b107 (relative clauses) and b10 (define the prose conditions) wait on this review.

Brainstorming of 2026-11-08 (the person: research, experimentation and prototyping, maybe a major release). Settled so far: definitions (types, "is a", relations) are timeless vocabulary; requirements are effect rules, structural rules, state invariants, transition rules and liveness, each a premise of the logic with its own verification route (scenarios, a check, judgement through a design-decision, runtime monitors by sampling); only ubiquitous lines need a route, carried by the decision (a `verified_by` beside `enforced_by`), never inside the sentence; a coined adjective is a state missing its structure (r33's `unreadable` becomes "a request comment does not conform to the comment schema"); formats live in external artifacts (a schema) that a line names and conforms to, read in as facts where a line needs their fields. Research plan, each phase a record: (1) CNL best practices and the field (Kuhn's PENS survey, Attempto, SBVR, EARS, FRET, GF's CNLs) beside a write-up of this grammar's underpinnings; (2) a sample of this context's lines and its grammar compared with those systems; (3) the full set of lines and conditions sorted by the structure that would replace each workaround.
