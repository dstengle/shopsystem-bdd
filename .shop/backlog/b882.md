# A line drafted into one capability can retire a requirement of another

## Notes

Found by b871-b873 (quote-118's move of laying's lines into applying): work line --retires <cap>#r<n> puts the new line into the retired line's own capability, so a line restated in another capability is retired with --no-line and written plainly, and the item no longer records which line replaces which. work batch start's batch_laid then counts the restatement as fresh, and gives the item back when its scenario passes before anything is built (b873: applying#r27, r28, r30, r31, r33, r34). work line <b> --into <capability> --retires <other cap>#r<n> '<line>' writes the line into the capability --into names and the pair under the retired capability's block, so the lay retires the id there and batch_laid reads the line as one that retires.
Rated high value and urgency, small: it unblocks b873 of the laying retirement chain, whose restated lines are otherwise given back at batch start; the change is the --retires check and pair across two capability blocks and batch_laid's fresh.

## Requirements

### drafting

#### [g113] A line drafted into one capability that retires a requirement of another is kept in the item
- [r113] When the person drafts an item with the requirement id of a requirement of a capability and another capability, the drafting service shall keep an item that holds a requirement of the capability that retires the requirement id.

### batches

#### [g193] A ready item whose line retires a requirement of another capability is not reopened when its scenario passes on the lay
- [r193] While an item is `ready` and the item meets the readiness bar and the item holds a requirement of a capability and a scenario of the item refers to the requirement id of the requirement and the scenario succeeds in a workspace of a batch and the requirement retires the requirement id of a requirement of another capability, when the person opens the batch, the batches service shall not reopen the item.
