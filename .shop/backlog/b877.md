# A line's parse is kept by what the line reads, so a new term re-parses only the lines it can change

## Notes

The person (quote-122): spec_parse_cache keys every parse by the sha of all grammar 4's sources (one generated Context module), so any term or model change misses all 869 lines, then batch_reached writes every feature and the corpus again. Key a line's parse instead by its footprint: the base grammar's files, the vocabulary entries of the words the line holds, the frames and senses of its verbs, and the kind tree, attributes, values and relation rows of the kinds those words reach (a new relation row adds readings of 'its' and 'the K of x' only for its kinds). A line whose footprint is unchanged keeps its parse with no compile; only the misses need the new grammar. batch_reached writes a feature and corpus block again only where a line's tree or form changed. Proved sound by a test replaying recent history: every footprint hit equals a fresh parse (the same trees, the same refusals). Optionally split Context per area so GF reuses unchanged modules. Before b874, after b876 gives the baseline (directive-11).
