# order-by on a collection: the form orders the collection, the scenario its members

## Notes

From the release 0.36.0 review (finding 3): for 'print a worktree that holds a file, ordered by version, in descending order' the form orders the worktree (ordered(x4 worktree, version)) while the generated scenario gives the files' versions and checks their order. One of the two is wrong: settle where order-by on a collection puts the order (decision 0093: the collection's members) and make the form say it.
Shaped 2026-11-16. Reproduced on a copy of grammar/corpus/constructs/spec: model --root <copy> parse --form "When the person runs `work start`, `work` shall print a worktree that holds a file, ordered by version, in descending order." prints response [x4: worktree, x5: file] in(x5, x4) print(e2, subject: x3, object: x4) ordered(x4, version, descending): the atom names the worktree, which has no version, while spec_lf_ordered.then gives three files in the worktree their versions and checks their order. Decision: the scenario is right and the form is wrong; the order after a collection is an order of its members, the Ks of the row K | in | Y (decision 0093: an order is said with its key, and the key is an attribute of the members; b328: the order on the Ks, not the collection). The form says it as order-by-every does: an every box over the members, restricted by in(x6, x4), with ordered(x6, version, descending) in its scope, beside the act on the collection (response [x4: worktree, x5: file] in(x5, x4) print(e2, subject: x3, object: x4) every [x6: file] in(x6, x4) scope ordered(x6, version, descending)); so an ordered atom always names a referent of a kind that has the attribute. Modules: scripts/spec_lf_ordered.py (np builds the box for r<n>_in_By_<T>, show, facts finding the act on the collection the box restricts by, member and then reading the members off the box; the held path of member goes), and the readers that must recognise this box as an order and not an every object of the response: scripts/spec_lf_facts.py / spec_lf_quant.py (no every object, no uall), scripts/spec_steps4.py / spec_steps4_boxes.py / spec_steps4_counter.py (no R6 Ks or counter-example of the box; the order steps stay spec_lf_ordered.then, unchanged in what they write); grammar/constructs.md regenerated (model constructs --write), grammar/language.md and docs/writing-requirements.md where they say the order-by form. Scenario: design-model-check runs model parse --form on the catalogue context's order-by example and checks that the ordered atom names a referent of the held kind (file) under an every box restricted by in(_, the collection), not the collection. Existing lines that must stay green: r303 (parse), the opposite-orders L1 (contradict), feature-file-tools r123, r127, r131 (the order-by scenario and its counter-example, unchanged). Left out: an order on a collection with no row K | in | Y (still refused); order-by-every unchanged. Depends on nothing. Rated medium value, medium urgency as the release review's finding gave; size raised from small to medium: the form change reaches facts and the steps' box readers, not one rule.

## Requirements

### reading

#### [g62] The logical form puts an order after a collection on its members
- [r62] When the person parses a requirement that uses `order-by` with the logical form, the language service shall return a logical form that uses an order of a member of a collection.

### specification model

```model
object collection
  relations: has → member (many)

object member
  relations: has → order (many)
```

- **collection** (noun) → object collection. A thing that a noun phrase of a requirement names and that holds members by a relation whose phrase is `holds`, as a workspace holds files.
- **member** (noun) → object member. A thing that a collection holds, as a file is a member of a workspace, and that an order said after the collection is of.
