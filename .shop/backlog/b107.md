# Object relative clauses on a verb's frame: a channel that a viewer subscribes to, the video list that the caller requests

## Notes

Left by b84: the verb-frames design (.shop/records/2026-11-07-verb-frames/design.md, What changes, spec_typed) lists noun-phrase modifiers 'a video that a caller requests'; none was built. feed's 3.0 upgrade report finding 3: its r4 ('when the channels a viewer subscribes to have no video, return an empty list') cannot be written, so feed added a one-place adjective quiet (adjective of viewer) as a workaround. With this, 'While no video is from a channel that a viewer subscribes to' parses, and a condition can be defined through it in the Def grammar.
Noun-phrase modifiers from a verb's frame, listed in the verb-frames design (What changes, spec_typed) and not built by b84: a noun followed by 'that', the verb's subject and the verb ('a channel that a viewer subscribes to', 'the video list that the caller requests'), the noun's kind the frame's object; in a requirement, in a condition's definition (Def grammar) and as facts for model logic, with the steps generated. A noun of another kind is refused naming the verb. Built after b106, as feed's case strands a prepositional verb's preposition. feed's report (finding 3) asks this, the person's paste in quote-17; with it feed can define its workaround adjective quiet through the clause.

## Requirements

### design-model-check

- **relative** (adjective of requirement): a requirement with a noun followed by `that`, a subject and a verb whose frame takes the noun's kind as its object, as in `a channel that a viewer subscribes to` or `the video list that the caller requests`.
- **misrelated** (adjective of requirement): a requirement with a noun followed by `that`, a subject and a verb whose frame does not take the noun's kind as its object.
- **clausal** (adjective of term): a condition whose definition has a noun followed by `that`, a subject and a verb, as in `no video is from a channel that the viewer subscribes to`.

#### [g68] `model parse` parses a noun with a relative clause
- [r68] When the person runs `model parse` on a requirement that is relative, `model` shall exit successfully.

#### [g69] `model parse` refuses a relative clause whose verb does not take the noun
- [r69] If the person runs `model parse` on a requirement that is misrelated, then `model` shall exit with an error naming the term.

#### [g70] `model check` reads a condition defined through a relative clause
- [r70] When the person runs `model check` on a term that is clausal, `model` shall exit successfully.
