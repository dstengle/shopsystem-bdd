# The order phrase takes a comma before what follows it

## Notes

From the feed product's session on 0.35.0 (feedback relayed by the person, 2026-11-16), item 6: '..., in descending order, to the caller' fails at 'to'; only '... in descending order to the caller' parses, and 'to the caller, ordered by ...' is refused.
Shaped 2026-11-16 (triage: shape, with b300/b302 as orders on any act; no quote of the person's: the item comes from the feed product's feedback). Reproduced on a copy of grammar/corpus/constructs/spec (its give: 'Frame: agent gives file to agent'): model --root <copy> parse 'When the person runs `work start`, `work` shall give every file, ordered by version, in ascending order, to the person.' prints 'does not parse at to (token 21 of 24)', may follow: file; the same line without the comma before 'to' parses (t_version_3_k_file_Every ... Ascending as give's object, the person its recipient); 'give every file to the person, ordered by version, in ascending order' does not parse at ordered. Change: an order (order-by, order-by-every) that stands before what follows it in its clause (a recipient, an oblique) closes with a comma, as an apposition does, and is linearised so; at the end of a clause it takes none. Decision: the comma-less form before a recipient is no longer read (alpha, no compat; one linearisation, so that the construct catalogue round-trips). Modules: scripts/spec_rgl_np.py (orders: the order phrase's closing comma, e.g. a field or a BIND token the slot's linearisation puts before what follows), grammar/constructs.md regenerated if the forms change (Form: '<np>, ordered by <attribute>, in ascending|descending order[,]'), grammar/language.md's order paragraph and docs pages that show an order before a recipient. Scenarios: design-model-check runs model parse on a requirement of the construct catalogue's context with give, its order followed by a closing comma and the recipient, once for order-by and once for order-by-every, and checks the exit. Left out: an order after the recipient ('to the caller, ordered by ...'), which stays refused (the order belongs to its noun phrase); language.md's wording of where an order stands (b302). Depends on b300 (delivered), whose order-by-every on a verb whose frames name the collection's kind the order-by-every scenario needs.
Rated: medium value, medium urgency and small size as the triage gave: the comma-less form already reads, so the feed's line has a rewording; one linearisation change in orders and two parse scenarios.
