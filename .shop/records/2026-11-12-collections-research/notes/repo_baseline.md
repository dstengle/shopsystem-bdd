## Q1. What the logical form represents: referents, atoms, boxes; every, counts, order, values, cardinality

### Takeaway
The logical form is a DRS-like structure of typed referents, flat atoms (`event`, `state`, `rel`, `attr`, `cmp`,
`history`, `distinct`, `exists`, `initiates`) and boxes (`not`, `every`, `only`, `count`, `number`, `or`) with a
restrictor and scope. It has no collection object, no ordering relation between things, no aggregate over values,
and no comparison of two things' attribute values: "order" is only an attribute holding a constant, and `cmp` compares
an attribute with a constant (or, in the time pack, a moment with a moment, which nothing downstream reads).

### Cited Findings
- A referent is `Ref(name, kind, origin, value, definite)`; origins are `while`, `trigger`, `response`, `indexical`,
  `parameter`, `constant` — [scripts/spec_lf_core.py:15, 38-44](scripts/spec_lf_core.py)
- An atom is `Atom(pred, args)` with pred `event`, `state`, `rel` (a relation row: has, holds, of, in) or `attr` — [scripts/spec_lf_core.py:4-8, 47-50](scripts/spec_lf_core.py)
- A box is `Box(op, refs, body, scope, count)`; ops `not`, `every`, `only`, `count` (with bound and number, e.g. ("at most", 1)), `number` (the Ks "the number of Ks" counts), `or` of `case`s, plus the rule parts while/where/when/if/response — [scripts/spec_lf_core.py:8-11, 53-59](scripts/spec_lf_core.py)
- A `Form` is modality + fixed referents + parts in order — [scripts/spec_lf_core.py:62-68](scripts/spec_lf_core.py)
- Determiners are read as `a`, `every`, `no`, `only`, `count`, `fixed`; an "and" list gives one atom per member, "or" an `or` box; "every", "no", "only" and a count wrap the rest of the clause — [scripts/spec_lf_core.py:71-92, 153-157](scripts/spec_lf_core.py)
- R6: "every K that P" in a response is an `every` box (restrictor, scope); the generator gives two Ks so the line is not met vacuously — [grammar/language.md:290-311](grammar/language.md)
- R7: "one K" is exactly one, "a K" at least one; "at least/at most/exactly/more than/fewer than n Ks" is a `count` box with bound, number, restrictor and scope — [grammar/language.md:313-345](grammar/language.md)
- "the number of Ks that P" is a referent `x: number` with a `number` box; compared ("is more than n") it is R7's count box with an empty scope; the Ks counted are made inside the box so no later "the K" binds to them — [grammar/language.md:1072-1087](grammar/language.md)
- The design's call 4 adopted Attempto-style collections: distributive "every K" / plural, counts, "each of the Ks" over a definite collection, and explicitly "No collective readings in 4.0 ('the tasks are done' means each task is done)" — [.shop/records/2026-11-09-cnl-4/design.md:232-244](.shop/records/2026-11-09-cnl-4/design.md)
- The design's form table lists `every` for "every, plurals in a response, each of" and says "'newest' and 'oldest' are an order over a definite collection" — [.shop/records/2026-11-09-cnl-4/design.md:486-495](.shop/records/2026-11-09-cnl-4/design.md); neither "each of" nor newest/oldest exists in the grammar or the logical form today (grep of `grammar/*.gf`, `scripts/spec_rgl*.py`, `scripts/spec_lf*.py` for newest/oldest/EachOf finds nothing), and known limit #10 "each of the Ks" confirms it is missing — [grammar/language.md:1978-1983](grammar/language.md)

- Ordered attributes: an attribute entry with `Order: by number.` or `Order: as listed.`; "the T of X is after v", "before v", "after v and before w" give `cmp(x, T, op, v)`; a code that is no value is refused; an attribute with no `Order:` has no after/before — [grammar/language.md:820-833](grammar/language.md); [scripts/spec_lf_order.py:1-13, 25-41, 58-90](scripts/spec_lf_order.py)
- The right side of `cmp` is always a point: a declared value (`_PtVal`) or a code — [scripts/spec_lf_order.py:43-51, 64-66](scripts/spec_lf_order.py)
- Known limit #6: "the language has no comparison of two things' values ('the release of the repository is after the release of the plugin'), which the check could not decide exactly over named points (design call 9)"; migration-tools r77-r80 say a constant release instead and get a new id when the release moves on — [grammar/language.md:1868-1874](grammar/language.md)
- The design's reason: every comparison in the repository's 265 lines was against a constant, so comparisons are exact over the named points plus gaps in clingo; "Z3 joins only if a line must compare two unknown values with an order (a date against a date), which no line here does" — [.shop/records/2026-11-09-cnl-4/design.md:303-306](.shop/records/2026-11-09-cnl-4/design.md); also Z3 allowed only beside clingo for ordered values — [design.md:116](.shop/records/2026-11-09-cnl-4/design.md)
- The time pack does compare two things: "x is after y", y a moment's referent, `cmp(x, after, y)`; "within the last N units" `cmp(x, after, now - N units)` — [scripts/spec_lf_time.py:1-10](scripts/spec_lf_time.py); [grammar/language.md:488-506](grammar/language.md). But `model logic` refuses a line holding one ("no translation of a comparison of moments") — [scripts/spec_lf_facts_atoms.py:17-18](scripts/spec_lf_facts_atoms.py), and `features generate` writes no scenario of it (known limit #16) — [grammar/language.md:2011-2018](grammar/language.md)
- TRIAL: with `imports: [time]` and *publication time* made `Kind of: moment`, "While a publication time of a video is after a publication time of another video, ..." parses to `of(x1,x2) distinct(x4,x2) of(x3,x4) cmp(x1) after x3`; `features generate` then refuses it: "latest-videos#r12: refused: no translation of a comparison of moments". Making publication time a moment also broke r7 ("the verb return takes as its object result or a kind of it"), because a kind has one place in one kind tree (spec_rgl_vocab: "the kinds in one tree under the base kinds" — CLAUDE.md module map row `scripts/spec_rgl_vocab.py`).
- Values and value kinds: an attribute's `Values (adjective):` / `Values (noun):` declare each value's kind; the short form "x is `v`" of a noun value is refused naming the full form "the T of the K is `v`" — [CLAUDE.md module map, `scripts/spec_value_kinds.py` row]; Term fields `values`, `order`, `value_kinds` — [scripts/spec_lexicon.py:34-58](scripts/spec_lexicon.py)
- Value sets: "is one of `a` or `b`" is an `or` box (a case per value), "is not `a` or `b`" a `not` box, "has no T" `T(x) = none` — [grammar/language.md:927-930](grammar/language.md); module map row `scripts/spec_lf_values.py`
- Relation rows: `| from kind | word | to kind | cardinality |`, an optional `Field of:` cell — [scripts/spec_relations.py:1-16](scripts/spec_relations.py). Cardinality has only one effect: a row is functional when the cell says "one", or is blank and the word is for, with, on, of or in — [scripts/spec_logic_vocab.py:17-25](scripts/spec_logic_vocab.py); functional rows become `fun/1` facts — [scripts/spec_logic_vocab.py:95](scripts/spec_logic_vocab.py). There is no "many", no ordered cardinality, no "list" cardinality.
- "the K of x" / "its K" over an `of` row is a functional image made once per line, kept outside a negated clause's `not` — [scripts/spec_lf_core.py:83-85](scripts/spec_lf_core.py); [scripts/spec_lf_np.py:173-189](scripts/spec_lf_np.py)
- Aggregates over values are absent: "the number of Ks" counts, but "the highest of a set, a sum, one above the highest" are known limit #3 — [grammar/language.md:1971-1976](grammar/language.md); ratio #18 — [grammar/language.md:1991-1995](grammar/language.md)
- Transitive closure ("in a thing at any depth") is absent — [grammar/language.md:2005-2009](grammar/language.md)

### Inferences
- A collection today can only be a kind (e.g. *video list*) related to its members by a relation row (`video | in | video list`); membership is then `rel(in, member, list)`, and the "every" box can quantify it. There is no notion that the list is *the* set of its members (no extensional equality, no closure "and nothing else" except by an `only` box).
- An order over things needs a comparison of two things' values (#6). The logical form already has the atom shape for it (`cmp(x, op, y)` with y a referent, from the time pack), so the representation gap is smaller than the downstream gap: `model logic` (clingo over named points) and `spec_steps4` have no reading of a referent on the right of `cmp`.

### Gaps
- Whether clingo over named points could decide two-thing comparisons was asserted undecidable "exactly over named points" by design call 9; I did not find a written analysis of what would be needed (Z3, or a finite order over the scenario's own referents).

## Q2. Constructs catalogue and known limits relevant to collections, ordering, "for each X its Y", ordered choice, top-n

The catalogue has quantity constructs (`every`, `bare-plural`, `the-plural`, `no-noun`, `count`, `one`, `number-of`, `two`, `same`, `another`, `different`, `only`, `ordered` against constants, `value-set`) but nothing for a collection's order, ordered choice ("the first K that P"), top-n ("up to n Ks in rank order"), distribution ("for each X its Y", "each of the Ks"), aggregates, or two-thing comparisons; each is a numbered known limit with the lines that worked around it.

## Q3. The generator: every (R6), counts, response referents and their restrictions, given blocks

### Takeaway
`spec_steps4` reads the form part by part: While/Where/trigger atoms become Givens, the trigger event the When, response items Thens in body order. An `every` box in a response becomes two named instances brought in by Givens with their restrictor; a count becomes the bound's number of instances. A new response referent is never brought in by a Given, and its restrictions are emitted as Thens before the act that introduces it (b268). Given blocks are parsed only as Given sentences and prepended verbatim, with no binding of their named instance to the generated referents.

### Cited Findings
- Module contract: Givens from While/Where and the trigger's non-event atoms (referent brought in at first mention), When from the event, Thens from response acts and conditions; "An `every` box gives two Ks, so the line is not met vacuously (R6): each brought in by a Given with its restrictor, its scope's Thens said of each" — [scripts/spec_steps4.py:1-19](scripts/spec_steps4.py)
- Naming: a referent is named by its kind's first letter and a counter in order of first mention; a later requirement's While/trigger referent of a kind and rank is the first one's — [scripts/spec_steps4.py:54-66, 81-86](scripts/spec_steps4.py)
- `acts`: an event or set atom is a Then; any other atom of the response (a restriction such as `order(x7) = ...` or `of(x4, x2)`) is a Then condition (`SCond`) in body order; `every` → `each` — [scripts/spec_steps4.py:204-225](scripts/spec_steps4.py)
- `each`: for copy 1 and 2, the box's refs renamed `<name>.<k>`, a `K_Given` each, the restrictor's Givens appended to `brought`, the scope's Thens; the second copy's acts marked `again` — [scripts/spec_steps4.py:227-238](scripts/spec_steps4.py)
- Scenario assembly: Givens + `r.brought` (deduplicated), then Whens, then Thens — [scripts/spec_steps4.py:271-300](scripts/spec_steps4.py)
- In a condition, "every K that P is Q" brings one K; a count brings the fewest Ks it admits (`least`: none for at most / fewer than; a parameter's "more than" one) — [scripts/spec_steps4_boxes.py:1-16, 33-40](scripts/spec_steps4_boxes.py); [scripts/spec_lf_core.py:215-221](scripts/spec_lf_core.py)
- In a response: `only` brings one K with its restrictor; a count brings the bound's number of copies (one past for "more than"), or for "at most n"/"fewer than n" n+1 / n copies with the Then on the last negated; a printed number brings two Ks — [scripts/spec_steps4_boxes.py:122-155](scripts/spec_steps4_boxes.py)
- An ordered comparison in a condition is a Given of one value on the right side of every point the first line names, a listed value first — [scripts/spec_lf_order.py:161-197](scripts/spec_lf_order.py); [scripts/spec_steps4.py:169-170](scripts/spec_steps4.py); any other `cmp` (two referents) is "no step of ..." — [scripts/spec_steps4.py:171](scripts/spec_steps4.py)
- Given blocks: a group's fenced `given` block is a list of (line, Block); the only allowed shapes are `the <words> <name> holds:` + docstring/table, or `... holds` + one code or quoted string; anything else is v15m — [scripts/spec_read_given.py:1-31](scripts/spec_read_given.py); docs: "Any other given line restates something the line already says ... The block becomes the scenario's first Given, data and all" — [docs/reference/capability-file.md:210-229](docs/reference/capability-file.md)
- The generator parses each block line with the step grammar, keeps only trees whose fun ends in `SCond`, `_Given`, `_HoldsBlock`, `_HoldsText`, and prepends them to every scenario of the group: `example + [...steps...]` — [scripts/features_generate4.py:20, 47-62, 87-89](scripts/features_generate4.py)
- batch-measurement g24: the block names "the measurement `m2`" (a code, one stage row, `"turns": 14`) — [spec/capabilities/batch-measurement.md:54-62](spec/capabilities/batch-measurement.md); the generated scenario then introduces "a measurement m1" with three stage rows s1-s3 and runs `measure show` on m1 — [features/batch-measurement.feature:31-48](features/batch-measurement.feature). Nothing relates `m2` to `m1` or its JSON rows to s1-s3; the docs show the same scenario as the model example — [docs/reference/capability-file.md:394-416](docs/reference/capability-file.md)

### Inferences
- The generator is an "abstract example" writer: it invents instance names and the existence of things, but never invents attribute values except where an ordered comparison forces one (spec_lf_order.given). It therefore has no machinery to give two members distinct values in a known order, nor to place them in a list in that order.
- Given blocks are the only place concrete data lives, and they are opaque: the instance a block names is a code, not one of the referents the line introduces, so a handler must reconcile two worlds by convention. Binding a block's name to a line's referent (by kind, e.g. "the measurement `m2`" ↔ the line's measurement) is the missing link.

### Gaps
- I did not read the batch-measurement handler to see how it reconciles `m2` and `m1` in practice.

## Q4. Declaration and definition mechanisms; could a "Let" type declaration fit the term-entry model?

### Takeaway
The lexicon is already a declaration language: typed entries (proper noun, noun, adjective, attribute, verb) with fields `Kind of:`, `This:`, `Values (...)`, `Order:`, `Frame:`, `Forms:`, `Schema:`, `Field of:`, `Redefines:`, plus relation rows with cardinality, and packs that layer them. A collection type ("a video list is a list of videos ordered by publication time, newest first") would fit as a new field on a noun entry, but nothing today reads an order over a kind's members, and the only ordered thing is an attribute's value domain.

### Cited Findings
- Entry fields read: `Values`, `Values (adjective)`, `Values (noun)`, `Transitions`, `Means`, `Forms`, `Frame`, `Plural`, `Not`, `Kind of`, `This`, `Redefines`, `Schema`, `Field of`, `Order` — [scripts/spec_lexicon.py:26-31](scripts/spec_lexicon.py); `Term` dataclass incl. `this`, `schema` (stored, checked by nothing yet, b241), `field_of`, `order`, `value_kinds` — [scripts/spec_lexicon.py:34-58](scripts/spec_lexicon.py)
- Parts of speech and their grammar slots: proper noun→actor, noun→kind, adjective→condition, attribute→attribute, verb→event — [scripts/spec_lexicon.py:78-80](scripts/spec_lexicon.py)
- A condition (adjective) has a definition sentence (its gloss) a `Def` parse reads — [scripts/spec_lexicon.py:214-218](scripts/spec_lexicon.py); but to the check a condition is prose: "grammar 4 reads no definition of it" — [scripts/spec_logic_vocab.py:10](scripts/spec_logic_vocab.py)
- `This:` names the run's own thing of a kind (R11, indexical referent) — [grammar/language.md:411-426](grammar/language.md); [scripts/spec_lf_core.py:4-6](scripts/spec_lf_core.py)
- Packs: `grammar/packs/<name>.md` and `spec/packs/<name>.md`, layered by `imports:` (each pack, then the glossary, then capability `## Terms`), `Redefines:` for a word a pack also defines — module map rows `scripts/spec_packs.py`, `scripts/spec_rgl_vocab.py` in CLAUDE.md; the time pack defines moment, duration, units, schedule, clock (with `This:`), reach — [grammar/packs/time.md:1-30](grammar/packs/time.md)
- `Order:` exists only on an attribute entry and orders the attribute's declared values (by number or as listed) — [scripts/spec_lf_order.py:25-41](scripts/spec_lf_order.py); in this repo only migration-tools' *release* uses it — [spec/capabilities/migration-tools.md:43-44](spec/capabilities/migration-tools.md)
- EXT's glossary already contains the intent in prose: **video list** "the videos `feed` returns to a caller, in the order it returns them. Kind of: result"; **order** (attribute of video list) "`newest-first` puts the most recently published video first. Values (noun): `newest-first`"; **publication time** a noun, Kind of: result; and a row `video | in | video list` with no cardinality — [EXT spec/index.md:44-49, 55-66]

### Inferences
- A "Let"/type declaration fits most naturally as fields on a noun entry, e.g. `Members: video (in).` and `Order: by publication time, newest first.` on *video list*, reusing (a) the existing `in` relation row for membership and (b) the existing `Order:` field name, extended from "values of this attribute" to "members of this kind by an attribute of theirs". For that, *publication time* must be an ordered attribute of video (`publication time (attribute of video) ... Order: by number`/a moment order), not a noun, which conflicts with EXT's current modelling where it is a returned `result` thing (r7).
- The adjective-with-definition mechanism is the closest existing "definition" construct, but its definition is unread by the check, so it cannot carry a checkable order today.

### Gaps
- Whether `Kind of:` allows multiple parents was only inferred from the TRIAL refusal and the module map's "one tree"; I did not read `spec_rgl_vocab.py` to confirm.

## Q5. Worked trace: EXT latest-videos r9 — where the intent is lost

### Takeaway
r9's intent (every video from the subscribed channel, in one list, newest first by publication time) is lost in three places: (1) the line can only say `order(list) = newest-first`, a constant with no link to members or publication times; (2) the logical form has no membership between the returned list and the videos and no order atom, and the returned list is a new referent unrelated to the requested one; (3) the scenario gives two videos with no publication times, never says the list holds them, and asserts the order constant before the list exists.

### Cited Findings
- The line: "[r9] While a viewer subscribes to a channel, when a caller with the YouTube access of the viewer requests a video list, `feed` shall return a video list whose order is `newest-first` to the caller and return every video from the channel to the caller." — [EXT spec/capabilities/latest-videos.md:26-27]
- Its form (`model parse --form`): `response [x7: video list]  order(x7) = \`newest-first\`  return(e2, subject: x6, object: x7, to: x3)  every [x8: video] from(x8, x2) scope return(e3, subject: x6, object: x8, to: x3)`; the requested list is `x5` in the trigger — output of `model --root EXT parse --form --file .../latest-videos.md`
- The generated scenario: Givens bring `a video list v2`, `a video v4` / `v5` each `from the channel c1`; When `the caller c2 requests the video list v2`; Thens `the order of the video list v3 is \`newest-first\``, `\`feed\` returns the video list v3 to the caller c2`, `\`feed\` returns the video v4 ...`, `... v5 ...` — [EXT features/latest-videos.feature:19-37]
- The glossary has the `video | in | video list` row r9 does not use, and *order*'s value is glossed in prose only — [EXT spec/index.md:45-46, 61]
- TRIAL: the membership half is expressible today: "... `feed` shall return a video list that holds every video from the channel to the caller." gives `response [x7: video list] every [x8: video] from(x8, x2) scope in(x8, x7)  return(e2, ..., object: x7, ...)`, and `features generate` writes `Then the video list v4 holds the video v3 / And the video list v4 holds the video v5 / And \`feed\` returns the video list v4 to the caller c2` (TRIAL features/latest-videos.feature, g5)
- TRIAL: "return the video list" in the response binds by R2 to the requested list (`return(e2, ..., object: x5, ...)`), so the requested/returned split (v2 vs v3) is the line's "a video list", not a necessity
- TRIAL: "a video list that holds only videos from a channel" parses to an `only` box (`only [x5: video, x6: channel] from(x5,x6) scope in(x5,x4)`), the "nothing else" closure
- TRIAL: "the newest video" does not parse ("does not parse at newest"); there is no superlative
- Thens of a response referent's restrictions precede its introducing act (b268) because `acts` emits non-event atoms in body order — [scripts/spec_steps4.py:204-213](scripts/spec_steps4.py); b268 — [.shop/backlog/b268.md:12-16](.shop/backlog/b268.md)

### Inferences
Loss points, in order:
1. **Line → lexicon.** "newest first" lives in *order*'s gloss; `newest-first` is a noun value with no semantics. *publication time* is a kind, not an ordered attribute of video, so even `cmp` against a constant is unavailable. The `in` row is unused, so "the list's videos" are not connected to "every video from the channel".
2. **Line → form.** `order(x7) = newest-first` is an opaque attribute equality; the `every` box's acts are separate `return` events to the caller, not `in(x8, x7)`; the form says nothing about any pair of members. x7 is a fresh response referent, not x5.
3. **Form → Gherkin.** v3 appears first in a Then (never a Given) and its order is asserted before it is returned (b268); v4 and v5 get no publication times, so the step `the order ... is newest-first` cannot be checked against data in the scenario; nothing says v4 and v5 are in v3, nor in which position; the handler of "the order of the video list v3 is `newest-first`" must embed the whole rule.
4. What a correct scenario would need: two (better three) videos with distinct publication times given in a known order (Givens with values, as spec_lf_order.given does for constants), a Then that the returned list holds each, and a Then on their positions ("v5 is before v4 in the list v3"), which needs an order/position atom in the form and a two-referent comparison step.

### Gaps
- I did not inspect EXT's step handlers (`tests/`) to see what "the order of the video list v3 is `newest-first`" actually checks.

## Q6. Scoping mechanisms below the glossary that a requirement-near "Let" (or an alternative to variables) could reuse

### Takeaway
Every binding mechanism today is line-local: R2's "the K" resolves only within one line's form (or to a `This:` indexical, which is context-wide), a capability's `## Terms` is pooled into one context lexicon, and a group shares only its title, its given block and, in the generator alone, While/trigger instance names by kind and rank. `model logic` never binds across lines by name; it searches unifications of each pair. A group-level declaration ("the video list" introduced once per group, then used in each line) would need a new antecedent source for R2 in `spec_lf_read.the`, a group-scoped referent kind in the form, the generator's sharing extended to it, and a decision on whether `model logic` treats it as a fixed shared referent or keeps unifying.

### Cited Findings
- **Capability `## Terms`**: the lexicon reads the glossary of `spec/index.md` and every capability's `## Terms` — [scripts/spec_lexicon.py:226-233](scripts/spec_lexicon.py); grammar 4's vocabulary takes "every entry of the glossary and of each capability side by side" — [scripts/spec_rgl_vocab.py:53-61](scripts/spec_rgl_vocab.py). Layers (pack, glossary, each capability's Terms) matter for `Redefines:` and precedence, not for scope — [scripts/spec_packs.py:8, 57](scripts/spec_packs.py). So a capability term is not capability-scoped in parsing: it is a context word defined in that file.
- **Group**: `Group(id, title, restates, given, requirements, line_no, odd)` — [scripts/spec_read.py:24](scripts/spec_read.py); the given block belongs to the group — [scripts/spec_read_given.py:34-50](scripts/spec_read_given.py) and is prepended to every scenario of the group — [scripts/features_generate4.py:87-89](scripts/features_generate4.py); the group title is the scenario name and the group's lines make one scenario — [docs/reference/capability-file.md:388-392](docs/reference/capability-file.md). The lines of a group are each parsed and translated alone: `forms = [(s.id, spec_lf.form(vocab, s.tree)) for s in mine]` — [scripts/features_generate4.py:75](scripts/features_generate4.py); `translate` makes a fresh `State` per tree — [scripts/spec_lf.py:29-36](scripts/spec_lf.py).
- **R2 ("the K")**: binds to the latest earlier accessible referent of kind K in the same line (not inside a no/not/every/only/count box or an or case), else to the image of a `K | of | S` row of the one earlier S, else to the run's own K by `This:`, else the line is refused ("no earlier noun phrase, functional relation or declared indexical gives ...") — [scripts/spec_lf_read.py:10-19, 56-82](scripts/spec_lf_read.py). Accessible origins are the parts' plus constant, indexical, parameter — [scripts/spec_lf_read.py:35](scripts/spec_lf_read.py). `st.refs` starts empty for each line — [scripts/spec_lf_core.py:97-107](scripts/spec_lf_core.py).
- **"this K" / `This:`**: a kind's entry with `This:` gives an indexical referent, "the run's own K", reachable from any line by "this K" or an unbound "the K" (R11) — [grammar/language.md:411-426](grammar/language.md); [scripts/spec_lf_read.py:72-73](scripts/spec_lf_read.py); `State.constant` makes one referent per (origin, value) per line — [scripts/spec_lf_core.py:114-119](scripts/spec_lf_core.py). It is context-wide and names one thing per run, not a description.
- **A definite with no antecedent**: `Ref.definite` ("said 'the' and bound to no earlier referent (the reading rules ... resolve it)") — [scripts/spec_lf_core.py:44](scripts/spec_lf_core.py); check.lp still reads `the(R,V)`: such a definite "is the one thing of its kind: when one side has one, each role of that kind the other side's pre-state or definites name is it" — [grammar/check.lp:124-126](grammar/check.lp). R2 now refuses such a line before it reaches the check unless `This:` or an `of` image supplies it.
- **Conditions defined by a sentence**: an adjective's gloss is its definition, read by a `Def` parse — [scripts/spec_lexicon.py:214-218](scripts/spec_lexicon.py), but "a condition (an adjective) is prose to the check: grammar 4 reads no definition of it" — [scripts/spec_logic_vocab.py:10](scripts/spec_logic_vocab.py); `every`/`no` are read "through a `Def`" in check.lp (CLAUDE.md module map, `grammar/` row).
- **Inline descriptions**: relative clauses and "whose" restrict a noun phrase in place ("a session that does not follow a stage", "a stage whose status is `done`", "whose" over an `of` row) — CLAUDE.md module map row `scripts/spec_rgl_rel.py`; one relative clause per noun: coordinated "whose ... and that ..." does not parse (TRIAL, Q2).
- **Cross-line binding in the generator**: `bind` names a later requirement's While and trigger referents as the first requirement's of the same kind and rank; response, constant and box referents are not shared — [scripts/spec_steps4.py:54-66](scripts/spec_steps4.py). Givens of While/Where and the When are taken from the first requirement only (`n == 0`) — [scripts/spec_steps4.py:279-288](scripts/spec_steps4.py); `r.pre` (what an ordered value Given must meet) is the first line's only — [scripts/spec_steps4.py:280-281](scripts/spec_steps4.py). So two lines' "a video list" in the trigger become one instance v2 by rank, but the response's returned list of each line would be a fresh name per line.
- **Cross-line binding in `model logic`**: each statement is translated to facts alone — [scripts/spec_context.py:81, 89-101](scripts/spec_context.py); pairs of requirements are found by a shared-vocabulary index, "never the cross product", and joined by unification ("each role of B is one of A's roles of a compatible kind, or its own instance") under the file's rule "never join two requirement-level facts on a vocabulary word" — [grammar/check.lp:4-8, 40, 106-116](grammar/check.lp); the triggers of a pair are one event when their doer and object unify — [grammar/check.lp:147](grammar/check.lp). Cross-line identity is therefore searched, not declared.
- **Entailment / explain**: a query's premises are the lines on its trigger (CLAUDE.md module map, `scripts/spec_entail.py`), again by matching triggers, not by shared names.

### Inferences
What a group-level declaration (a description given once per group, then "the video list" in each line) would take:
1. **Concrete syntax and reading.** A group-level block (e.g. beside or in the `given` fence, or a `Let:`/`Where:`-like line under the group heading) parsed as a noun phrase with its restrictions ("a video list that holds every video from the channel ...") by the existing NP grammar; `spec_read.Group` gets a field for it — [scripts/spec_read.py:24](scripts/spec_read.py).
2. **Logical form.** A new referent origin (say `group`) beside `ORIGINS` — [scripts/spec_lf_core.py:15](scripts/spec_lf_core.py); `translate` seeded with the group's referents and their restriction atoms before the line's own — [scripts/spec_lf.py:29-36](scripts/spec_lf.py); `spec_lf_read.the` gains one more fallback (after line referents and `of` images, before `This:`) — [scripts/spec_lf_read.py:56-82](scripts/spec_lf_read.py); `ACCESSIBLE` extended — [scripts/spec_lf_read.py:35](scripts/spec_lf_read.py). The design question is whether the declaration's restrictions are a presupposition (a precondition each line inherits, as While would be) or a definition (the referent *is* the described thing, e.g. the returned list); for a returned collection it is the latter, i.e. a response-side referent shared by lines, which no origin today models.
3. **Generator.** Extend `bind`'s sharing from While/trigger-by-rank to group referents by identity — [scripts/spec_steps4.py:54-66](scripts/spec_steps4.py) — and place their restriction Givens/Thens once; the given block could then be bound to the declared referent (its `<name>` code ↔ the group referent), closing the m2/m1 gap of Q3.
4. **`model logic`.** Either translate the group referent as a constant-like term shared by its group's lines (so pairs within a group join on it, which bends the check.lp rule "never join on a vocabulary word" only if it is keyed by group, not by word), or leave it as an ordinary referent and keep unification, which already lets the lines agree. The second needs no change to check.lp but loses the declaration's guarantee that the lines speak of one thing.
5. Alternatives to explicit variables that reuse what exists: (a) a group-scoped `This:`-like indexical ("the video list" with no antecedent = the group's declared one), a small generalisation of R11; (b) R2 extended across a group's lines in order (a later line's "the K" binds to an earlier line's referent), the discourse reading ACE uses across sentences; (c) keep one line per group carrying the description and let other lines say "the K", which (b) would make legal.

### Gaps
- I did not test whether a group whose lines share a response referent by rank would need `spec_steps4.bind` to include response origins, nor how `model logic`'s L1/L6 pair search would treat a group-shared constant; these are design questions with no code to cite.
