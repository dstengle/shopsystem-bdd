I've written your agreement on "may" outcomes as decision 0127 and noted it on b546 "Saga checks".

On sagas: no, and b546's title makes that mistake by lumping three different things under "saga". A saga is a business transaction that spans several steps in other contexts, can't be made atomic, and keeps consistency by **compensating** earlier steps when a later one fails. It lives over time, with its state in the core: the order's lifecycle in the example.

Most core activities that call out are not sagas:

| core activity | example | a saga? |
|---|---|---|
| a query | browsing counts availability | no: nothing changes, nothing to undo |
| a single command whose outcome the core reacts to | withdraw an order: free the reservation | no: one step, and a refusal is just an outcome to handle |
| a notification or event sent out | notify the shopper | no |
| a long-running process waiting on replies, nothing to undo | a document sent for approval, waiting days for the reply | no, but it needs a deadline or a way out |
| several state-changing steps across contexts, where a later failure must undo earlier ones | place an order: reserve stock, secure the charge, take, collect | **yes** |

So b546's three checks apply to different sets:
- **Every outcome handled** applies to every request the core makes through a port, saga or not. It is decision 0127.
- **The lifecycle terminates** applies to every long-running process: any object of the core that waits on replies between statuses.
- **Compensation covered** applies only to sagas.

On terms, I propose:
- **port request**: any request the core makes through a driven port;
- **long-running process**: an object of the core whose lifecycle waits on replies (DDD's process manager);
- **saga**: a long-running process with two or more state-changing steps in other contexts and compensations.
