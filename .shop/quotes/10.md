You assumed there's a base grammar that every line follows. There is one, but it's only half as strict as your shorthand.

**What the base grammar already makes strict**

- **Nouns are typed.** Each kind in a glossary (channel, video, caller) gets its own slot, so a video can't stand where a channel is expected.
- **Prepositions are typed per pair of kinds.** The relation row `video | from | channel` is the only way to say "a video from a channel", and a phrase that could attach two ways is refused.
- **"A" and "the" work roughly like your variables.** "A channel" introduces one, and "the channel" refers back to it when the line is turned into facts.

**What it leaves loose: the verbs**

Every event shares one untyped category, and three generic patterns take any event:

- **verb alone:** "a caller asks"
- **verb and any object:** "give a video"
- **verb and two objects:** "give the caller a video"

Nothing records that *request* takes a video, or that *return* needs one. That's how r2 got through.

There's also no negative relation. "A user is subscribed to a channel" can be said today with a `user | subscribed to | channel` row. "A user is **not** subscribed to a channel" can't, which is partly why feed fell back on a one-place condition.

**Your shorthand, as I read it:**

- Every clause is *noun | verb | noun*.
