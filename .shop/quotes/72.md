### the person, 2026-11-24T17:01:50.822000+00:00, 6610775e-1501-464e-838a-f8ae5e84c3ba, cited

```text
There is something I'm having trouble putting into words. The core uses ports to talk to dependencies. The ports fulfill the needs of the core. The adapter needs to implement against the contract(s) of another upstream services. These services may or may-not publish full contracts, although the internal services will. Fulfilling the requirements lines for a port is a technical implementation, but for internal services there will be a full contract available with its own capabilities and requirements. The concept of an adapter mapping was simplistic because it relied on simple 1:1 operations. There does need to be some kind of mapping of downstream requirements to upstream contract. At the very least for internal services this is needed for the context to understand when it needs to request a capability addition or change.

So how do we document the design of the adapter? The requirements are set by the port and generate gherkin scenarios, but the implementation needs design that doesn't fit in the core. This could be an ADR or a more specific adapter design document. This is a situation where an isolated instance of requirements language and behavioral tests on technical behavior would be useful.

This line of thought has me thinking about technical requirements vs business requirements. A bit of searching turned up CALM, Archimate  and the C4 model as well as the old standby, UML.

Short term, there needs to be some kind of design artifact or artifacts for a port adapter, including how requirements are implemented and how they map to upstream services.

Longer term, research needs to be done on architecture description languages to bridge the gap between the core and actual implementation.
```
