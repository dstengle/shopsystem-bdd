**What a vocabulary or model change sets off at opening:**
1. **Grammar 4 is generated and compiled again.** It's cached by its sources, so any new term or model line misses the cache.
2. **Every requirement line is parsed again.** The parse cache is keyed by the grammar's sources, so all 869 lines miss it at once. They're parsed by at most 8 worker processes (a quarter of the machine's cores).
3. **Every feature and the whole corpus are written again,** because `batch_reached` regenerates all 23 features once the grammar's inputs change.

A chore batch skips all three. What both kinds still pay is the per-item rule check (a clingo solve before and after) and the run of the laid scenarios. My guess is that step 2 is the largest, but I haven't timed it.
