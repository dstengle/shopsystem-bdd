That fits what we already have. Capabilities already have most of a job's shape: a capability's `narrator:` is an actor and its `purpose:` is a motivation. Your rule promotes one capability per job to coordinator. Here is a concrete shape to react to.

**The link points from the solution up to the problem.** The product declares the job; the coordinating capability claims it.

```
product/jobs.md
  j3  the person
      When work is ready, the person wants it built and reviewed together,
      so they can merge one reviewed change.

spec/capabilities/batches.md   (frontmatter)
  coordinates: j3
```

I'd put the link on the capability, not the job, for two reasons:
- The product stays in problem terms and never names a capability. Capabilities get renamed, moved or split far more often than jobs change.
- It matches decision 0129, where context ids hang off the product rather than the shop, so a context can move without the product changing.

**What a job is tied to, derived rather than declared:**
1. the coordinating capability's lines: the job's entry points and its outcome;
2. every line those lines reach through their requests, which we can already compute:
   - driven ports and their port lines;
   - adapter specs and the lines they realise;
   - the upstream contracts' lines, followed by the request chain from b545.

So the full set of lines for a job is a trace computed from the coordinator. It is never a list someone maintains by hand.

**Checks at the product level:**
- each job has exactly one coordinating capability;
- that capability's `narrator:` is the job's actor;
- each job's coordinator reaches every subdomain the job is mapped to;
- a coordinating capability claims at most one job, or several. That last one is open, see below.
