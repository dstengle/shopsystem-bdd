```text
I agree. "Area" is doing at least five jobs, and the contracts work is about to give it two more. In this repository it currently means:

1. **A part of the business, classified core, supporting or generic.** That is DDD's *subdomain*. The six model files (`analysis`, `execution`, `measurement`, `specification`, `tracking`, `upgrades`) are classified this way through `kind:`.
2. **A model file**, `spec/model/<area>.md`, with its objects, services and events.
3. **A vocabulary layer**, `spec/vocabulary/<area>.md`, the words a line in that part may use (v_m1 checks this).
4. **Where a capability belongs**: the `area:` line in every capability file, and the `| area | kind | capabilities |` table in `spec/index.md`.
5. **A driven port.** b548 just made "an area of kind `port`", and that's where the conflation shows. A port isn't a part of the business at all, so `kind: core | supporting | generic | port` mixes a business classification with an architectural role.

The adapter-spec design then adds a "provider area" (the upstream's words, as seen by an adapter) and an "adapter area" (the adapter's own objects, such as its entry store).

**Proposed terms**

| today | proposed | why |
|---|---|---|
| area of kind core / supporting / generic | **subdomain** (its `kind` stays core, supporting or generic) | it is DDD's term for exactly this, and its classification |
| area of kind port | **driven port**: its own concept, not a kind of subdomain | it is an architectural role, the core's need of something outside |
| model file, vocabulary layer | the subdomain's (or port's) **model** and **vocabulary** | the files belong to a subdomain or a port; nothing needs a separate name |
| a capability's `area:` | `subdomain:` | the capability serves one subdomain |
| provider area (in an adapter spec) | the **provider's model** | the upstream's words as the adapter sees them |
| adapter area (in an adapter spec) | the **adapter's model** | the adapter's own store and technical things | […]
```
