**The shape**
- A driven port is an area of the downstream's model, in its own words, and its port contract is a capability. Each port line carries `Relied on by:`, naming the core lines that depend on it.
- Each adapter is a nested context, `adapters/<port>-<provider>/`, with its own spec. It reads the port from the parent and holds the provider's pinned contract, or a description of it marked `assumed`. It also holds the adapter's own requirement lines (the realisation lines) and an `adr/` folder.
- The trace, the realises check's result and drafted requests are derived, not written by hand.

**Ten design decisions (D1–D10)** cover:
- the downstream keeping its own terms;
- the core orchestrating, and adapters never deciding business;
- the port contract being bounded by the core;
- each adapter being a nested context;
- the upstream owning its contract's detail;
- a port line counting as met when the adapter's lines plus the provider's lines entail it;
- mechanisms living in adapter ADRs;
- conformance tests binding an adapter, and the trace never binding it;
- assumed provider lines counting as evidence only when run against the provider;
- saga replies being told apart by the core's statuses.

**Two tracks, as you asked**

  - L-1: a response can make a request with arguments.
  - L-2: one actor across two vocabularies.
  - L-3: entailment that follows a request across lines.
  - L-4: state invariants (b542).
  - L-5: saga checks — every outcome handled,  […]
